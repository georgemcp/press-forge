# Trim Proof Business Blueprint

*Last updated: 2026-06-15*

## Executive Position

Trim Proof should not compete as another generic AI flyer, card, or poster maker. Adobe, Canva, Design.com, Venngage, and template libraries already own much of that mental shelf. Trim Proof's sharper wedge is:

> Turn a plain-English print brief into a checked PDF/X proof, with AI creative upstream and deterministic prepress downstream.

That message gives buyers a reason to care beyond "AI made a design." It also lets Trim Proof sit between two crowded categories: generic AI design tools that stop at screen-ready assets and expert preflight tools that assume a production file already exists.

## ICP

Primary ICP:
- Small print shops that receive incomplete customer files and want faster first-pass setup.
- In-house marketers who create local print collateral but do not know prepress.
- Freelance designers and small agencies that need safer handoffs for low-margin print jobs.

Secondary ICP:
- Restaurants, real estate teams, event organizers, local service businesses, and creators that need repeat flyers, menus, postcards, posters, letterhead, or business cards.

Anti-ICP:
- Enterprise packaging teams, arbitrary PDF repair buyers, approval-routing teams, and buyers who require guaranteed printer acceptance without vendor review.

- "Turn a print brief into a checked PDF/X proof."

Messaging pillars:

## Marketing Psychology

Use these models ethically:
- Jobs to Be Done: Customers hire Trim Proof to reduce print-file uncertainty, not to "use AI."
- Status-quo bias: Show that users can start with a watermarked proof and do not need to abandon Canva or their printer.
- Loss aversion: Frame avoidable rework, rejected files, and missed print deadlines without fearmongering.
- Social proof: Do not invent logos. Build proof through pilots, screenshots, and documented preflight reports first.
- Activation energy: Give sample briefs, default formats, and a clear "generate dummy proof" first step.
- Paradox of choice: Keep pricing to three paths and recommend Pro only for recurring print jobs.

## Sales Enablement

Core talk track:
- "Trim Proof is not trying to replace every design or prepress tool. It is the faster bridge from print brief to checked proof for common local print jobs."

Discovery questions:
- How many print files do you prepare or reject each month?
- Which jobs fail most often: flyers, cards, menus, brochures, postcards, posters, or letterhead?
- What causes the most rework: bleed, crop marks, low-resolution images, color, fonts, or client copy changes?
- Who decides whether a file is production-ready?
- What would a faster first-pass proof be worth per month?

Objection handling:
| Objection | Response | Follow-up |
|-----------|----------|-----------|
| "We already use Canva." | Canva is a strong design start. Trim Proof is for the printer-facing proof and PDF/X-oriented handoff. | Which Canva exports fail most often? |
| "We already have Acrobat/PitStop." | Keep them. Trim Proof starts earlier by helping non-specialists create a checked starter proof. | Who prepares files before prepress sees them? |
| "Will this guarantee printer acceptance?" | No. Printer specs still rule. Trim Proof makes common structural issues visible before export. | Which specs should we support first? |
| "The price is too low for shops or too high for small users." | Single export handles occasional jobs; Pro is for recurring work. Pilot feedback will tune packaging. | How many exports would make Pro obvious? |
