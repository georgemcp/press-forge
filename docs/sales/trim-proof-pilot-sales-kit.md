# Trim Proof Pilot Sales Kit

*Last updated: 2026-06-15*

## One-Page Pilot Offer

**Headline:** Brief to checked PDF/X proof for common local print jobs.

**Who it is for:** Small print shops, freelance designers, and in-house marketers who handle repeat flyers, menus, postcards, posters, brochures, business cards, or letterhead.

**Problem:** Print files often look finished on screen but still fail quiet production checks: bleed, crop marks, safe area, font handling, image DPI, color workflow, PDF/X status, or unclear handoff notes.

**Trim Proof approach:** AI helps upstream with the creative start. Deterministic prepress code owns the proof structure, including trim, bleed, safe area, crop marks, vector text, color workflow, image DPI checks, and PDF/X-oriented export.

**Pilot offer:** 10 export credits for qualified shops or repeat print teams that will test real supported jobs and share feedback.

**What the pilot includes:**
- Free watermarked demo proofs for supported products.
- 10 clean export credits for real test jobs.
- Founder onboarding call.
- Feedback follow-up on supported products, printer specs, report clarity, and missing workflow needs.

**Honest boundary:** Trim Proof reduces avoidable file-prep uncertainty. It does not guarantee printer acceptance, replace production judgment, repair every existing PDF, or support arbitrary dielines.

**Primary CTA:** Join the print-shop pilot at `/for-print-shops`.

## Discovery Call Script

**Opening:** Thanks for taking a look at Trim Proof. I want to understand where print-file prep breaks down for you before showing anything. If it is not a fit, we will say that plainly.

**Qualification questions:**
- How many small-format print jobs do you prepare, reject, or revise in a typical month?
- Which jobs create the most rework: flyers, cards, menus, posters, brochures, postcards, or letterhead?
- What usually causes the first rejection or cleanup pass: bleed, crop marks, low-res images, fonts, color, client copy, or wrong size?
- Who prepares the first file before a production specialist sees it?
- Do customers usually send native design files, Canva exports, PDFs, screenshots, or only rough notes?
- What would make a starter proof useful enough to test on real work?

**Demo flow:**
- Show the homepage positioning: AI creative upstream, deterministic prepress downstream.
- Open `/prepress-checklist` and connect the checklist to their current rejection reasons.
- Open `/for-print-shops`, `/for-marketers`, or `/for-designers` based on persona.
- Create or describe a watermarked demo proof for one supported product.
- Explain what paid export unlocks and where printer-specific review still matters.

**Close:** The next useful step is not a big contract. Pick one repeat job type, run it through a demo proof, and tell us which checks or shop-specific settings would make it more useful.

## Objection Handling

| Objection | Why they say it | Response | Follow-up |
|-----------|-----------------|----------|-----------|
| "We already use Canva." | Canva is familiar and fast for layouts. | Keep Canva where it helps. Trim Proof is for the printer-facing proof path: PDF/X-oriented export, vector text, bleed, crop marks, color workflow, and preflight report. | Which Canva exports fail most often? |
| "We already have Acrobat, PitStop, or a RIP." | They have expert tools and do not want replacement risk. | Good. Trim Proof starts earlier, before the expert tool, when the buyer or junior staff only has a brief or rough file. | Who touches the file before prepress sees it? |
| "Will my printer accept it?" | They fear promising too much. | Printer specs still rule. Trim Proof makes common structural checks visible, but final acceptance depends on the printer, substrate, finishing, and upload rules. | Which printer spec should we compare against first? |
| "AI designs are too generic." | They have seen low-quality AI output. | Trim Proof is not selling AI as the final authority. AI can help with creative assets; deterministic code controls text, boxes, bleed, crop marks, and proof checks. | Which part of the job needs creativity, and which part needs production control? |
| "This is too lightweight for our shop." | Production teams may need deeper repair tools. | That may be true for complex jobs. The pilot is for common starter products where a cleaner first proof can save handoff time. | Which simple job repeats enough to justify a trial? |

Reply with "pilot" and the first job type you want to test.

## Rep Notes

- Do not claim customer logos, acceptance rates, or time-saved metrics until they exist.
- Do not position Trim Proof as a universal repair product.
- Lead with the checklist and the visible proof report, not AI novelty.
- Ask for one real job, not a broad workflow migration.
