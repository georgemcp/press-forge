# Trim Proof Pilot Outreach Playbook

*Last updated: 2026-06-15*

Use this playbook for the Week 2 founder-led pilot push. The job is to recruit ten qualified pilot participants without inventing proof, overclaiming production outcomes, or treating every lead like the same buyer.

## Outreach Goal

Recruit ten qualified pilot participants from a twenty-five-account target list.

Qualified means the person can test at least one supported product: flyer, poster, menu, brochure, business card, postcard, or letterhead. The best early participants already feel pain around bleed, crop marks, safe area, type handling, image DPI, color workflow, PDF/X status, or unclear handoff notes.

Do not count a lead as recruited until they have agreed to test a real supported job, accepted the pilot terms, and have a next step logged in the admin pilot pipeline.

## Target List Design

Build the first list manually. Do not scrape private emails, invent contacts, or add companies only because they look prestigious.

The first public-source starter batch lives in `docs/sales/trim-proof-pilot-target-list-2026-06-15.md`. Treat it as sourced targets only: no row counts as recruited until outreach, pilot agreement, and a next step are logged in the admin pipeline. If current launch-list records are smoke, test, or internal addresses, do not count them; backfill with public-source targets and label the launch-list gap.

Target mix:

| Segment | Target count | Why it matters | Best source |
|---------|--------------|----------------|-------------|
| Local print shops | 10 accounts | They see rejected customer files and can validate handoff clarity. | Google Maps, local chambers, printer websites, LinkedIn company pages. |
| Freelance designers | 6 accounts | They handle small client jobs where production cleanup eats margin. | LinkedIn, portfolio sites, local design groups, referral asks. |
| In-house/local marketers | 5 accounts | They repeat flyers, menus, cards, posters, and postcards without prepress expertise. | Local business groups, restaurants, real estate teams, venue/event teams. |
| Existing launch-list leads | 4 accounts | They already showed intent and can be prioritized by source. | Trim Proof admin pipeline and email signup source. |

Required fields before outreach:

| Field | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| Company or person | Use public business identity or the signup email context. |
| Segment | `print_shop`, `designer`, `marketing_team`, `checklist_reader`, `account_signup`, or `general_launch`. |
| Source | Use the real source, such as `print_shop_page`, `designer_page`, `prepress_checklist`, LinkedIn, referral, Google Maps, or local group. |
| First supported job | The product they are most likely to test first. |
| Likely pain | Bleed, crop marks, low-resolution images, color workflow, PDF/X, file intake, or client copy changes. |
| Public contact path | Email form, public email, LinkedIn profile, referral intro, or community comment thread. |
| Last signal | Signup date, public post date, referral date, or date added. |
| Follow-up status | Start as `needs_follow_up`; update after every touch. |

## Prioritization

Use this order when time is limited:

1. `pilot_print_shop` or `print_shop_page` signups in the admin pilot pipeline.
2. Print shops with visible file-prep, design, prepress, or wide-format services.
3. Designers who mention print, restaurant menus, real estate collateral, event collateral, or brand handoff.
4. Checklist readers who ask about bleed, crop marks, PDF/X, or Canva exports.
5. General launch-list leads with no company context.

High-priority leads should have one clear reason for contact. If that reason cannot be written in one sentence, the lead needs more research before outreach.

## Daily Operating Cadence

Use a ten-business-day push.

The Day 2 first-touch batch is staged in `docs/sales/trim-proof-day-2-first-touch-batch-2026-06-15.md` and in the admin dashboard's "First-touch batch" panel. It is ready-to-send copy only; keep prospects as `needs_follow_up` until the founder actually sends and logs each email.

| Day | Work block | Output |
|-----|------------|--------|
| 1 | Build the twenty-five-account list and tag segments. | List ready, no outreach yet. |
| 2 | Send the first ten direct messages or emails. | Ten first touches logged. |
| 3 | Send five more first touches and reply to responses. | Fifteen first touches logged. |
| 4 | Send remaining first touches. | Twenty-five first touches logged. |
| 5 | Follow up with non-responders from day 2. | Follow-up touch logged. |
| 6 | Book or run setup calls. | Call notes and first supported jobs logged. |
| 7 | Follow up with non-responders from day 3 and 4. | Follow-up touch logged. |
| 8 | Help active pilots run one dummy proof or export-credit job. | Job type and blockers logged. |
| 9 | Ask active pilots for report clarity feedback. | Evidence intake started. |
| 10 | Decide which participants count as recruited and which leads should be blocked, nurtured, or revisited. | Ten-pilot gap known. |

Do not mark the Week 2 launch task complete until recruited participants exist outside the doc.

## Message Matrix

### Print Shop Owner Or Production Lead

**Subject:** Pilot: checked PDF/X proofs for small customer jobs

Hi {{name}},

I am building Trim Proof for common local print jobs where the file looks done on screen but still needs bleed, crop marks, safe area, image DPI, color workflow, or PDF/X checks before handoff.

The pilot is simple: ten export credits, founder onboarding, and one feedback call around a real supported job like a flyer, menu, card, postcard, poster, brochure, or letterhead.

It does not guarantee printer acceptance or replace your production judgment. The goal is to see whether a checked starter proof and report make customer-file cleanup clearer.

Would one repeat job type be worth testing?

### Freelance Designer

**Subject:** Pilot for small print-job handoffs

Hi {{name}},

I am inviting a small group of designers to test Trim Proof on low-margin print jobs where the design is mostly decided but the handoff still needs trim, bleed, crop marks, safe area, type, image, and PDF/X checks.

The pilot includes ten export credits and founder onboarding. A good test job is a real flyer, menu, postcard, business card, poster, brochure, or letterhead.

Trim Proof is not a replacement for your design judgment. It is meant to be a production safety layer before the file goes to a printer.

Is there one client print job you would want to try it on?

### In-House Or Local Marketer

**Subject:** Quick pilot for recurring local print collateral

Hi {{name}},

I am opening a Trim Proof pilot for teams that create repeat local print assets like flyers, menus, cards, postcards, posters, brochures, or letterhead.

The product turns a plain-English brief into a checked PDF/X-oriented proof, with visible bleed, crop marks, safe area, image DPI, color workflow, and report notes before a clean export.

The pilot includes ten export credits and a short setup call. It does not guarantee every printer will accept a file; printer specs still control final approval.

Would it be useful to test one upcoming print job?

### Existing Launch-List Or Checklist Lead

**Subject:** Want to test one real Trim Proof job?

Thanks for joining the Trim Proof list.

I am recruiting the first pilot participants now. The useful next step is one real supported job: flyer, menu, card, postcard, poster, brochure, or letterhead.

If you are a fit, I can set you up with ten export credits and a short onboarding call. In return, I will ask what the proof report made clearer, what still needed review, and which printer-specific settings matter most.

Reply with the first job type you want to test and the printer spec you normally check against.

## Admin Pipeline Logging

Every touch should leave enough context for the next follow-up.

Use the admin dashboard's "Add pilot prospect" form for founder-sourced targets before they sign up. The dashboard stores the manual target, merges it with any later signup or paid-order signal by email, and keeps the status visible in the pilot pipeline.

Use the "First-touch batch" panel for the first ten manual sends. Open the draft in the founder mailbox, send it manually, then click `Log sent`. The button records a `first_touch_sent` row in `pilot_outreach_events`; it does not send email.

Map outreach fields to the current pilot pipeline:

| Admin field | How to use it |

| `companyName` | Public company name or signup-provided company. |

## Pilot Evidence Logging

After a participant runs a real supported job, use the admin dashboard's `Record pilot evidence` form. This is separate from outreach logging: outreach says what communication happened; evidence says what the product handled, what still needed review, and whether anything can later support public proof.

Capture:

| Evidence field | How to use it |
|----------------|---------------|
| `email` | Same prospect email used in the pipeline. |
| `jobType` | Flyer, poster, menu, brochure, business card, postcard, or letterhead. |
| `testedPath` | Dummy proof, export credit, Pro, or unknown. |
| `sourceMaterial` | Brief, screenshot, PDF, Canva export, image, or other starting point. |
| `printerSpec` | Vendor spec or workflow requirement used for comparison. |
| `checksSummary` | Checks that passed, checks that needed attention, and missing product support. |
| `reportClarity` | Whether the preflight report made the next action clearer. |
| `outcome` | Reviewed only, needs revision, used after review, not a fit, or blocked. |
| `quotePermission` | None, anonymous, or attributed. |
| `publicClaimStatus` | Not approved, internal only, or approved public. |
| `productVersion` | Commit, release, or version tested. |

Do not publish a pilot learning, quote, customer name, or outcome until quote permission and public claim status both support the intended use. A row marked `approved_public` can support bounded launch proof; it still cannot support broad acceptance-rate, time-saved, or replacement claims by itself.

## Objection Replies

| Objection | Short reply |
|-----------|-------------|
| "We already use Canva." | Keep it. Trim Proof is for the printer-facing proof and report layer after the creative start. |
| "Our RIP catches problems." | That is useful. Trim Proof is meant to catch and explain common issues before the file reaches the RIP or expert prepress step. |
| "Will it be accepted by my printer?" | Printer specs still rule. The pilot is about making common checks visible, not promising universal acceptance. |
| "We need packaging/dielines." | That is outside the first pilot. Trim Proof is focused on supported small-format products first. |
| "We do not want AI designs." | The production layer is deterministic. AI can help upstream, but proof geometry, text, crop marks, and checks are code-controlled. |
| "Send me proof it saves time." | We are collecting that evidence in pilots. For now, the safe claim is clearer handoff checks, not measured time savings. |

## Evidence And Claim Safety

Before using any pilot result publicly, check `docs/business/claim-evidence-register.md`.

Collect:

- Job type tested.
- Source material.
- Printer or vendor spec used.
- Checks passed and checks requiring review.
- Whether the proof report made the next action clearer.
- Time spent only if the participant can explain the method.
- Quote permission and attribution level.
- Whether the output was used, revised, rejected, or reviewed only.

Never publish:

- Customer names, logos, or quotes without permission.
- Acceptance-rate claims without evidence.
- Time-saved metrics without measured before/after data.
- Guarantees of printer acceptance.
- Claims that Trim Proof replaces Canva, Acrobat, PitStop, RIPs, or printer review.

## Success Criteria

Week 2 is working if:

- The twenty-five-account target list exists with segments and likely first jobs.
- Every first touch and follow-up is logged.
- At least ten qualified pilot participants agree to test one supported job.
- Each recruited participant has a next step, first job type, and follow-up status.
- Evidence intake has started for any participant who runs a dummy proof or clean export.

If fewer than ten participants agree, do not soften the metric. Record the gap, strongest objections, best-performing segment, and next sourcing channel.
