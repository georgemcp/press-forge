# Press Forge / Trim Proof Design Context

## Design Context

### Users
Trim Proof serves small print shops, in-house marketers, freelance designers, local business owners, restaurant teams, real estate teams, event teams, and agencies that need recurring print collateral without deep prepress expertise. They are usually trying to move from a rough brief, customer PDF, screenshot, Canva-style export, or loose job note into a checked proof for flyers, posters, menus, brochures, business cards, postcards, or letterhead.

### Brand Personality
The interface should feel precise, useful, calm, rigorous, and approachable. The product earns trust by showing what it checks, what it does not claim, and where printer-specific judgment still matters.

### Aesthetic Direction
Use an editorial production-tool feel rather than a decorative AI-generator feel. The visual world should borrow from proof sheets, print-shop job tickets, crop marks, measured layouts, preflight reports, and clean production notes. Keep the workspace dense enough for repeat use, while public pages should stay clear, concrete, and buyer-aware.

### Design Principles
- Lead with the proof surface: show trim, bleed, safe area, PDF/X status, reports, and product screenshots before abstract promises.
- Keep claims bounded: no fake logos, no universal repair language, no printer-acceptance guarantees, and no invented time-saved metrics.
- Make the first action obvious: create a demo account, run a watermarked proof, join the pilot, or read the checklist.
- Use restrained confidence: strong typography, measured spacing, practical icons, and visible evidence instead of hype.
- Preserve accessibility and trust: readable contrast, keyboard focus, honest empty states, and no hidden feature explanations that contradict visible behavior.
